# From Informal Thought to Formal Knowledge: SOTA Methods for LLM-Based Encoding into Typed Graphs and Dependent Type Theory

## TL;DR
- **Three mature but largely separate research streams** address your pipeline: (1) LLM ontology learning / typed-KG construction (OntoGPT/SPIRES, LLMs4OL, Text2KGBench, schema-constrained extraction validated by SHACL), (2) neural autoformalization into proof assistants (Lean/Coq/Isabelle, using the type checker as a verification oracle, benchmarked on miniF2F/ProofNet), and (3) neuro-symbolic semantic parsing (NL→FOL/typed lambda terms). The "use the checker as a correctness oracle" pattern is shared across all three and is your strongest architectural anchor.
- **The bridge you actually need — RDF/W3C graph semantics reconciled with MLTT/CIC dependent types — is genuinely under-explored**, but there is a direct precedent: Lai, Ng, Wong, See & Lin (arXiv:2003.03785, authors at NVIDIA AI Technology Centre Singapore, SUTD, and IHPC A-STAR), who describe their work as "a proof of concept for future works in this direction" reproducing RDF and SPARQL "in a unified manner with dependent type theory" in Coq — treating URIs as constructors, queries as types, and answers as proof-carrying witnesses. This is the closest existing work to your Atomic-Data-to-CIC stack and the right intellectual starting point.
- **Practical recommendation:** adopt a staged, human-in-the-loop pipeline — competency questions → schema-constrained structured extraction (Atomic Schema as the contract, enforced by Pydantic/instructor-style validators and SHACL-equivalent checks) → optional lifting of a typed core into MLTT/CIC where you need proof-relevant reasoning. Use the Atomic Schema validator as your first oracle and the type checker as your second; treat both open-world (graph) and closed/intensional (type theory) layers as distinct and explicitly mediated.

## Key Findings

### 1. LLM-based ontology learning and KG construction is now a structured subfield with benchmarks
- **LLMs4OL** (Babaei Giglou, D'Souza, Auer, ISWC 2023, arXiv:2307.16648) frames ontology learning as three tasks: term typing, taxonomy discovery, and non-taxonomic relation extraction. Their conclusion: foundational LLMs alone are "not sufficiently suitable for ontology construction that entails a high degree of reasoning skills and domain expertise" but work as fine-tuned assistants. The **LLMs4OL 2024 challenge** (arXiv:2409.10146) drew 272 submissions from 14 teams. The best term-typing result confirms how strong classical methods remain: RWTH-DBIS reported "by combining BERT with rule-based methods, we achieved an F1-score of 0.9938, surpassing GPT-4 [0.9264] and securing the first place for term typing on the Wordnet dataset." For taxonomy discovery, SKH-NLP took first place on Task B SubTask B.1–GeoNames with an F1-score of exactly 0.6557, using fine-tuned LLaMA-3-70B + BERT-Large. **LLMs4OL 2025** added a Text2Onto task and found hybrid pipelines (commercial LLMs + domain-tuned embeddings + fine-tuning + RAG) strongest, with specialized domain models leading in biomedical/technical datasets.
- **OntoGPT / SPIRES** (Caufield et al., Bioinformatics 2024, btae104) is the most directly relevant production tool: it takes a LinkML schema + free text and recursively prompts an LLM ("Structured Prompt Interrogation and Recursive Extraction of Semantics") to populate instances conformant to the schema, grounding extracted entities to ontology identifiers, and emits JSON/YAML/RDF/OWL. This is the canonical "schema-constrained generation + external grounding" pattern, and its accuracy is reported as "comparable to the mid-range of existing Relation Extraction methods" with the advantage of zero-training-data customization.
- **Text2KGBench** (Mihindukulasooriya et al., ISWC 2023, arXiv:2308.02357) is the key benchmark for ontology-driven KG generation: given an ontology + sentences, extract facts respecting concepts, relations, and domain/range constraints. Two datasets (Wikidata-TekGen: 10 ontologies/13,474 sentences; DBpedia-WebNLG: 19 ontologies/4,860 sentences) and seven metrics including precision/recall/F1, ontology conformance, and subject/relation/object hallucination measures. Baselines (Vicuna-13B, Alpaca-LoRA-13B) showed high ontology conformance but substantial headroom on fact-extraction F1.
- Other notable methods: **Ontogenia** (metacognitive prompting), **NeOn-GPT** (LLM pipeline over the NeOn methodology), **OntoChat** (conversational competency-question elicitation), **RevOnt** (reverse-engineering competency questions from KGs), and schema-adaptable/dynamic-schema extraction (AutoSchemaKG, LKD-KGC). The 2025 survey "LLM-empowered knowledge graph construction" (arXiv:2510.20345) organizes the field into schema-based vs. schema-free paradigms across construction, extraction, and fusion stages.

### 2. Typed/schema-respecting extraction and validation
- The dominant validation pattern is **SHACL/ShEx as a correctness oracle** on extracted triples. Systems like OntoLogX (cybersecurity logs, arXiv:2510.01409) and OntoMetric (ESG, arXiv:2512.01289) generate triples then enforce SHACL constraints (cardinality, type consistency, required fields); xpSHACL (arXiv:2507.08432) adds RAG-based natural-language explanations of violations. The literature distinguishes schema-level validation (required fields, valid predicates, cardinality) from semantic-level verification (type correctness, factual accuracy) and notes that most pipelines enforce only the former, leaving semantic validation to manual review.
- **Structured-output libraries** make schema-first extraction routine: **instructor** (built on Pydantic, >3M monthly downloads) lets you define a typed schema and get validated objects with automatic retries when validation fails; **LangChain's** Pydantic output parsers / LLMGraphTransformer and **LlamaIndex** offer similar capabilities; constrained decoding (llama-cpp-python JSON-schema grammars) guarantees structural validity at generation time. This is the engineering substrate for Atomic-Schema-constrained extraction.
- **GraphRAG** (Microsoft, open-source) builds entity-centric KGs with community detection and LLM-generated community summaries; **Graphiti** (Zep) is a temporally-aware KG memory layer (on Neo4j/FalkorDB) for agents that incrementally integrates updates and invalidates (rather than deletes) contradicted facts. Both target retrieval/agent memory rather than formal typing, but are the leading practitioner frameworks for LLM-driven graph construction.

### 3. Neural autoformalization uses the type checker / kernel as the verification signal
- The foundational pattern is **Draft-Sketch-Prove** (Jiang et al., ICLR 2023): an LLM drafts an informal proof, maps it to a formal sketch with "holes" (open goals), and an automated prover closes the holes. Variants: subgoal-based demonstration learning (arXiv:2305.16366), POETRY (recursive decomposition), and SPADER (sketch, prove, add detail, repeat).
- **Benchmarks:** miniF2F (Olympiad-level, cross-system) and ProofNet (371 examples / 297 statements of undergraduate pure math — real/complex analysis, linear/abstract algebra, topology — originally Lean 3; Azerbayev et al., arXiv:2302.12433, which introduced *prompt retrieval* and *distilled backtranslation* and reports *typecheck rate*, the proportion of samples accepted by Lean's type system, as a key metric).
- **Current proving SOTA** (Lean-verified, reliable): DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B reaches 88.9% pass@8192 on miniF2F-test (and 82.4% with only 32 CoT samples): "reaching 88.9% pass ratio on the MiniF2F-test and solving 49 out of 658 problems from PutnamBench" (Ren et al., arXiv:2504.21801); Kimina-Prover-72B 80.7% pass@8192 (arXiv:2504.11354); Goedel-Prover-SFT 57.6% pass@32 and #1 on PutnamBench at the time (arXiv:2502.07640); InternLM2.5-StepProver 65.9% miniF2F-test and 27.0% ProofNet pass@256 (arXiv:2410.15700); DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 63.5% miniF2F / 25.3% ProofNet (arXiv:2408.08152).
- **Crucial caveat on autoformalization (translation) metrics:** statement-translation "accuracy" numbers are badly inflated when judged by LLMs. Herald reports 96.7% accuracy on miniF2F-test via its back-translation LLM-judge, "compared to 73.0% for InternLM2-Math-Plus-7B and 50.1% for TheoremLlama" (Gao et al., Herald, ICLR 2025) — but the independent audit "miniF2F-Lean Revisited" (arXiv:2511.03108) found that "when we perform a human review of the outputs of Herald @128 that LLM has marked as 97% correct, we arrive at a much lower accuracy of 66%," and that the Herald+Kimina end-to-end pipeline yields only 34.8% effective accuracy after comparing final proofs to the original informal statements. **The type checker verifies that a formal statement type-checks and that a proof is valid — it does NOT verify that the formalization faithfully captures the informal intent.** This faithfulness gap is the single most important lesson for your pipeline.
- **Semantic/round-trip verification** addresses faithfulness: symbolic equivalence (using ATPs to check logical equivalence of premises/conclusions) + semantic consistency (back-translate the formal statement to NL and compare embeddings); Li et al. (arXiv:2410.20936, NeurIPS 2024) combine both for up to 0.22–1.35x relative improvement on MATH/miniF2F. "Don't trust, verify" (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) grounds quantitative reasoning via autoformalization. Type-theory-based provers (Lean, Coq, Agda, Isabelle) are explicitly the prominent target family in this literature.

### 4. Semantic parsing into formal logic / typed representations
- The dominant neuro-symbolic pattern uses the **LLM as a semantic parser** that maps NL to FOL/ASP/Prolog, then hands off to a sound symbolic solver, which acts as a "reliable oracle once supplied with correct input syntax." Key systems: **LINC** (Olausson et al., 2023, NL→FOL → theorem prover), **Logic-LM** (Pan et al., 2023, self-refinement from solver feedback), **SatLM**, and compositional approaches (Divide-and-Translate, arXiv:2410.08047) that decompose complex statements for more faithful FOL translation. The recurring finding is that "an LLM cannot faithfully perform complex first-order logic translation," which limits these pipelines — again, the translation/faithfulness step, not the downstream solving, is the bottleneck.
- CCG-style typed semantic parsing and NL→lambda-calculus are the classical precedents; the modern trend folds these into LLM-based translation with solver verification.

### 5. The bridge problem: RDF/DL semantics vs. dependent type theory
- **The core tension is well-characterized.** OWL/RDFS use open-world, subsumption-based description-logic semantics (OWL 2 DL builds on SROIQ(D); class subsumption maps to FOL ∀x.C(x)→D(x)); MLTT/CIC is intensional, constructive, proof-relevant, and (in proof assistants) effectively closed with decidable type-checking. The open-world assumption corresponds naturally to intuitionistic/constructive logic (absence of proof ≠ proof of absence via the BHK interpretation and Curry-Howard), which is exactly the MLTT setting — this is the deep connection your stack can exploit.
- **Direct precedent — Dependently Typed Knowledge Graphs** (Lai et al., arXiv:2003.03785): reproduces RDF and SPARQL inside the Calculus of Inductive Constructions in Coq. URIs become primitive constructors / defined constants; RDF triples group entities and relations into type families (each family a type, members its terms); SPARQL queries become record types whose primary fields coerce to a parent type and whose secondary fields carry proofs of the query conditions; answers are proof-carrying witnesses, delivering provenance and explainability, with proof-assistant tactics automating witness construction. The authors explicitly position it as "a proof of concept for future works in this direction." This is the closest existing work to your exact stack.
- **Earlier type-theoretic ontology work:** Dapoigny & Barlatier ("A Type-Theoretical Approach for Ontologies," using the Extended Calculus of Constructions and dependent record types, demonstrated to scale on the SUMO foundational ontology) and Zhaohui Luo's type theory with coercive subtyping. These show classes/relations/roles can be defined precisely as dependent types — directly relevant to mapping Atomic Schema Classes and Properties.
- **HoTT angle:** univalence ("isomorphic structures can be identified") is philosophically attractive for KGs where owl:sameAs / owl:equivalentClass identify entities, but no mature KG-on-HoTT system exists — this remains open research.
- There is sharp practitioner work modeling OWL's OWA and SHACL's CWA in Agda, illustrating the constructive-logic correspondence directly: OWA ↔ intuitionistic/proof-theoretic reasoning, CWA ↔ decidability/classical reasoning, with dependent types providing a formal bridge between the two.

### 6. The faithfulness/verification loop is the unifying methodology
Across all three streams the winning pattern is identical: generate with an LLM → check against a formal oracle (SHACL for graphs; the type checker/kernel for type theory; an ATP/solver for FOL) → feed errors back for iterative refinement. The oracle guarantees structural/logical validity but NOT semantic faithfulness to the original intent, so a second, semantic check (back-translation, human-in-the-loop, competency-question testing) is required. Strikingly, even gold-standard human formalizations are imperfect: ReForm (arXiv:2510.24592) found via expert annotation on its ConsistencyCheck benchmark that 16.4% of miniF2F and 38.5% of ProofNet human-written formal statements themselves contain semantic errors — a sobering calibration point for any "ground truth" you build.

## Details

### Atomic Data as the mapping target
Atomic Data (atomicdata.dev) is a strict subset of RDF that is type-safe and extensible via Atomic Schema. Its serialization, JSON-AD, uses keys that are URLs of Atomic Properties; each Property links to a Datatype (ensuring static type-safety), a shortname (an ORM-friendly key), and a description. Key divergences from generic RDF that matter for your pipeline: Atomic Data **guarantees subject-property uniqueness**, adds a native **Array** datatype for ordered data, has a native event/state-change model (**Atomic Commits**, cryptographically signed for auditability), supports value enumeration via `allows-only`, and deliberately **does not support named graphs** (all statements should be retrievable by fetching the subject). Crucially, Atomic Schema is a closed-leaning schema/validation language — the docs state it "can maybe be compared to Atomic Schema" alongside SHACL and ShEx, and explicitly that "OWL is not designed for schema validation," while OWL's "open-world philosophy and focus on reasoning abilities can confuse developers." This means your target already leans toward a more closed, type-safe, validation-oriented model than classical OWL DL — which makes the bridge to intensional type theory *more natural* than for full OWL. AtomicServer ships a built-in ontology editor and serializes to JSON-AD and to RDF formats (Turtle, N-Triples, JSON-LD, RDF/XML), so round-tripping to the broader RDF tooling ecosystem (and to SHACL validators) is feasible.

### How the three streams map onto your stack
- **(a) Informal NL → typed graph:** Use SPIRES/OntoGPT-style schema-constrained recursive extraction with Atomic Schema as the LinkML-equivalent contract; ground extracted entities to existing Atomic resources/URIs; validate with Atomic Schema's validator (your SHACL analog). Competency-question-driven design (OntoChat/NeOn-GPT) defines what the schema must answer before extraction begins.
- **(b) Typed graph → MLTT/CIC:** Follow the Dependently Typed Knowledge Graphs recipe — map Atomic Properties/Classes to type families, resources to terms, and Atomic queries to record/Σ-types whose proof fields encode constraints. The Curry-Howard "queries-as-types, answers-as-witnesses" view gives you provenance for free, and Atomic's subject-property uniqueness and mandatory datatypes make the encoding cleaner than for arbitrary RDF.
- **(c) Reasoning/verification:** Use the type checker as the oracle for the intensional layer and Atomic Schema/SHACL for the graph layer; use autoformalization-style back-translation for semantic faithfulness.

### Prompting and architectural patterns worth adopting
- Iterative/recursive extraction with explicit **entity-then-relation decomposition** (SPIRES; Iterative Zero-Shot prompting, arXiv:2307.01128, which found decomposing into entity extraction then iterative triplet extraction more reliable than direct triple extraction).
- **Schema-constrained / constrained decoding** (grammar- or JSON-schema-constrained sampling via instructor/llama-cpp) to guarantee structural validity at generation time; automatic re-ask on validation failure.
- **Retrieval-augmented extraction** (pull relevant schema fragments / exemplars into context) — the consistently strongest LLMs4OL/KG paradigm.
- **Confidence/uncertainty handling** via self-consistency sampling plus combined symbolic + semantic candidate scoring (sample k candidates, rank by ATP-equivalence and back-translation similarity).
- **Type checker / kernel feedback loops** (Lean/Coq compiler errors fed back to the LLM), as in DeepSeek-Prover RL pipelines and the Lean Workbook / IndicMath active-learning loops where Lean errors become iterative feedback.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Schema-first, graph-native (build now).** Implement informal-note → Atomic Data extraction using a SPIRES/OntoGPT-style recursive, schema-constrained prompt with the Atomic Schema as the contract and instructor/Pydantic-style validation with automatic retry. Ground entities to existing URIs. Treat the Atomic Schema validator (and, via RDF export, a SHACL validator) as oracle #1. *Threshold to advance:* extraction precision/recall, ontology-conformance, and hallucination rates stable on a held-out set of your own notes, measured with Text2KGBench-style metrics.

**Stage 2 — Competency-question-driven schema evolution.** Use OntoChat/NeOn-GPT-style LLM elicitation to generate, filter, and cluster competency questions, and RevOnt-style reverse engineering to extract CQs from the graph you've already built; keep a human in the loop for validation. The consistent finding across LLMs4OL/OntoChat work is that *evaluation and validation*, not generation, is the bottleneck — and that prompting domain experts effectively is itself an undervalued skill.

**Stage 3 — Lift a typed core into MLTT/CIC where proof-relevance pays off.** Do not formalize everything. Identify the sub-domain where you need constructive proof, provenance witnesses, or decidable consistency, and apply the Dependently Typed Knowledge Graphs encoding (Lai et al.) in Lean/Coq/Rocq. Use the type checker as oracle #2. *Threshold:* only lift content whose schema is stable and whose reasoning needs exceed what SHACL/Atomic Schema validation already provides.

**Stage 4 — Faithfulness verification loop (non-negotiable).** For any NL→formal step (graph or type theory), add round-trip back-translation + an embedding/LLM-judge semantic check AND a human spot-check, because the autoformalization literature shows decisively that checker-passing ≠ faithful (Herald: 96.7% LLM-judge vs. 66% human; end-to-end effective accuracy 34.8%). Adopt the symbolic-equivalence + semantic-consistency candidate-scoring scheme (Li et al., arXiv:2410.20936): sample k candidates, rank, and surface the disagreement to a human reviewer.

**What would change these recommendations:** If your reasoning needs turn out to be purely subsumption/consistency checking, stay in the OWL/SHACL/Atomic-Schema world and skip MLTT entirely — the cost of a CIC lift is not justified. If you need proof-carrying provenance, decidable constructive reasoning, or dependent constraints that description logic cannot express (e.g., quantities indexed by other data, invariants across resources), the CIC lift becomes worth its substantial cost.

## Caveats
- **The RDF↔dependent-type bridge is research-grade, not production-grade.** Dependently Typed Knowledge Graphs (2020) is an explicit proof-of-concept in Coq, not a maintained toolchain; you would be building on thin ice and should budget for original engineering and theory work. No off-the-shelf system maps Atomic Data (or general RDF) into MLTT/CIC today.
- **Autoformalization faithfulness metrics are widely inflated** by LLM-judges (Herald ~96.7% LLM-judge vs. ~66% human; arXiv:2511.03108). Never trust a single checker-pass — structural or even proof-level — as evidence of correct *meaning*.
- **Even human "ground truth" formal statements contain semantic errors** (16.4% miniF2F, 38.5% ProofNet per ReForm/arXiv:2510.24592), so calibrate expectations for any gold standard you construct and prefer multiple independent checks.
- **Open-world vs. intensional semantics is a genuine impedance mismatch**, not just an engineering detail: OWL/RDFS assume OWA and extensional class semantics; MLTT/CIC is intensional and proof-relevant. Atomic Data's more closed, type-safe stance partially eases this, but identity (owl:sameAs vs. propositional/definitional/HoTT equality) and the meaning of negation differ fundamentally and must be handled explicitly at the bridge.
- **Benchmark numbers are moving targets.** Many cited proving/autoformalization results are 2024–2026 arXiv preprints (some very recent); treat exact percentages as snapshots, and note that proving pass-rates are Lean-verified and reliable while translation "accuracy" figures are not.
